About

Learning by building, not just by reading

Puviyarasu is a final-year B.Tech Artificial Intelligence and Data Science student at CMS College of Engineering, Tamil Nadu, drawn to the parts of computer science where logic meets ambiguity — distinguishing signal from noise, real from fake, useful from clever.

His first serious project, a fake news classifier, set the tone: rather than treat machine learning as an abstract subject, he wanted to see if a model could actually hold up against messy, real-world text. That instinct — test the idea, don't just study it — carries into how he approaches every new tool and concept.

Outside of pure software, he's just as comfortable with the physical side of computing: assembling hardware, troubleshooting networks, and understanding systems from the circuit board up. That dual fluency — theory and hands-on tinkering — is part of what makes his problem-solving practical rather than purely academic.

His vision is straightforward: become an engineer who can take an AI idea from a rough hypothesis to a working, deployed system — not just a notebook that proves a point.

Fake News Detection

A text classification project built to answer a simple but timely question: can a model reliably tell real news from fake? It became Puviyarasu's entry point into applied machine learning — moving from theory straight into a dataset, a pipeline, and a working classifier.

Problem

Misinformation spreads faster than fact-checking — a way to flag likely-fake articles automatically was needed.

Approach
